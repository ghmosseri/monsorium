II. — About the Artist

The Heretic

I. The name

G.H. is a name loaned from the titular narrator of Clarice Lispector’s novella, “The Passion, According to G.H.” The book is ensconced in what is leavened by Julia Kristeva in 1980, her psychoanalytic theory of ‘Abjection’ in her seminal work, “Powers of Horror”. Mosseri—“of Moses”, patrilineal surname—is borrowed from the heretic’s own experience of escape as a larger existential mechanism that serves as one’s prerogative “right to leave.”

II. The pseudonym

The heretic comes up with the pseudonym of G.H. Mosseri, the name serving as a symbolic plateau toppled in Kristevan reversal: “Bahala na,” or to leave it up to the gods, is not only impractical to Mosseri, but also impassionate.

III. The conduit

Moises was the human conduit for the miracles of Yahweh; thus, it is he who divides the sea, bathes the river in blood, and kills the Egyptian first-born through Him. He bears the sin of God’s wrath, unloading the guilt of God from His shoulders, within which he becomes his own abject—his own not-“I”—such that he dispels the God-ridden guilt from his body, the guilt he is forced to bear.

IV. The guilt

Nevertheless, this guilt is his to carry, this not-Moises constellation of agony, for the poverty of his spirit lies not in his capacity (or lack of it) to carry said guilt, it lies in his capability to keep the guilt separate from him, cementing it as outside of him—all the more it pulverizes him, all the more it beseeches him.

V. The roach

Similarly, G.H. encounters the roach in a pristine wardrobe. Overcome with abjection, she saddles herself in disgust. This putrid insect is not me. This putridity is beyond me. She nonetheless eats the insect after crushing it. It becomes part of her that fascinates and dishevels her.

VI. The heretic

G.H. Mosseri, then, is not an innocent monument to Kristeva. The heretic superimposes themselves against the Abject in every composition of art and literature they find themselves creating. Kristevan in such a way wherein their fascination in that which unsettles them becomes the locus of what they create.

VII. The sacrament

As such, they engage in the secular masochism that Kristeva had identified: erotic mortification is a virtue that leads G.H. into a faux paradise of real signifiers. Their art, then, is a foremost ode-like attribution towards that which serves as the one to sanctify them, and destroy them altogether.
